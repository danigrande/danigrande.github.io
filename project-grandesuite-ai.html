AI in GrandeSuite Next

A finance-grade ERP where AI reads supplier invoices, suggests accounts and payments, and flags anomalies — always as a suggestion, never as a decision, and never as a chatbot.

Applied to: GrandeSuite Next (in development) Status: Live in the product (MVP) Guardrail: advisory-only

This is the AI layer of GrandeSuite Next — the entry-level ERP I designed and built solo with AI agents. It is a deliberately narrow use of AI: it reduces manual work and error in specific, checkable places, and stays out of everything that touches money.

Every AI feature here answers one question: does it meaningfully reduce human effort or error? Not "is it cool?" If it doesn't pass that test, it isn't in the product.

3 Reading Supplier Invoices

Invoices arrive as PDFs, scans and photos. The system reads them with a two-tier approach: a fast path extracts text directly from native PDFs, and a vision model handles scanned documents and images. If the model is not available, a heuristic extractor takes over — so the flow never dead-ends.

Extraction returns the vendor, invoice number, dates, totals and every line item with its SKU. The header is then matched to a known vendor, and lines are linked to the purchase order by SKU before the three-way match runs.

4 Confidence & Human Review

Extraction is not trusted blindly. Every field carries a confidence score, and the interface shows exactly which values to check. Low-confidence data is what the human review step is for — the system learns from corrections but never fully trusts itself.

The correction panel places the scanned document beside the extracted values, with per-field confidence badges and the match result in one view. A person confirms or fixes the data; the corrected values become the record of truth.

Same pattern as the chatbot: AI produces output → an automatic check scores it → the uncertain cases go to a human. It is the same discipline, applied to money instead of banter.

5 Suggesting GL Accounts

For each invoice line, the system suggests a general-ledger account with a confidence score. It reads the line description and the tenant's own chart of accounts, and proposes the most likely mapping. The accountant accepts, edits, or ignores it — the suggestion is a starting point, not a posting.

6 Payment Recommendations & Anomalies

Every day the system proposes which bills to pay and which to delay, given cash on hand, and flags the things a human would otherwise miss:

What to pay, and when

A ranked recommendation of pending bills against the available cash balance, with the reasoning in plain language.

Duplicates

Probable duplicate invoices are paired and surfaced before they are paid twice.

Anomalies

Outliers and unusual amounts are flagged with a short explanation of why they look wrong.

It never moves money. The controller reviews the recommendation and approves a payment run. AI proposes; a person disposes.

7 Explaining Deviations

When a number deviates from expectation — a variance, an unusual balance, an exception — the system writes a one-or-two-sentence plain-English explanation of what likely caused it and what to do next. It turns an alert into something a busy person can act on in seconds.

8 The Setup Copilot

Onboarding is where ERPs usually stall. During setup, AI helps with the fiddly parts:

  • Account mapping — suggests how an incoming chart of accounts maps onto GrandeSuite's.
  • Duplicate detection — finds duplicate customers and vendors by name and email before they pollute the data.
  • Barcode classification — classifies unknown items so the catalogue starts populated.

All of it is advisory: the person setting up confirms each suggestion.
