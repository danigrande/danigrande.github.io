AI Quality

Evals & Human in the Loop

How I keep AI output trustworthy at scale — measuring quality automatically, and routing the hard cases to a human.

Part A: Agente Mundial (live) Part B: GrandeSuite Next (in development)

LLM-as-a-Judge Golden Dataset Human Review Quality Gate

AI products fail quietly: a response is plausible but wrong, a personality drifts, a document is misread. You can't eyeball quality at scale — and you can't fix what you don't measure. So every AI output gets scored, and anything suspicious goes to a human.

This page covers two very different products, each in its own part. Part A — Agente Mundial, a chatbot that talks to people. Part B — GrandeSuite Next, an ERP where AI touches money. The pattern is the same; the stakes are not.

1 Why Evals

  • Plausible-but-wrong is the default failure mode of language models — it reads fine and costs trust.
  • Consumer products can't ship unmeasured AI. One bad response can damage the brand; at scale, bad responses are guaranteed unless you filter them.
  • Evals are the test suite of AI — the same discipline as software tests, applied to probabilistic output.
  • What gets measured can be improved: scores feed prompt calibration and grow the golden dataset.
Part A · Live

Agente Mundial — a chatbot that talks to people

Every reply is scored by a second, smaller model before it reaches the group, and the hard cases go to a human. The quality machinery runs on every message, in 14 languages.

2 LLM-as-a-Judge

A smaller, faster model evaluates every reply in the same pipeline as generation. It scores two axes — language purity (0–10) and quality (0–10) — and every score is logged with the response's latency and tokens.

  • The judge model is a smaller LLM (llama-3.1-8b-instant) — cheap enough to run on everything.
  • A rule-based fast-path catches script contamination first: if a Spanish response is mixed with Hangul, Arabic, or Cyrillic, a regex check fails it at 0/10 without even calling the model.
  • Judging is sampleable — an EVAL_SAMPLE_RATE controls how much traffic is judged (default 100%), so the quality gate can be throttled under load.
  • The quality gate retries: a failed response regenerates up to three times, with the judge's feedback injected into the next attempt. After three failures it is "force-approved" and flagged for human review — never silently dropped, never silently shipped.

Calibration is the hard part. The judge and human reviewers disagreed on borderline responses. A structured rubric with examples improved alignment — but it remains an ongoing calibration problem, not a solved one. These targets are a contract to meet, not a score already achieved: the measurement infrastructure exists, but automated alerting and rollback are still missing.

3 The Golden Dataset

102 test cases across six categories — including adversarial cases built to break the system:

An eval runner scores the model against the dataset and persists pass rates per category, so regressions are visible run over run — the golden dataset is the test suite, and the judge is what runs it.

What it does — and doesn't — do. The golden dataset validates the model; it does not train it. It catches regressions and steers prompt engineering, but it is not a fine-tuning set (see section 7).

4 Human in the Loop

Humans don't review everything — they review the right things. Human reviews are created automatically when:

  • A user downvotes (rating 1–2) — the response goes to review.
  • A calibration signal fires — a high user rating (4–5) that the judge disagreed with.
  • The quality gate exhausts its retries — a response force-approved after three failed attempts.

Guardrails on the review itself: a daily cap of 20 reviews, deduplication (max 3 similar reviews per personality + language per week), and a reviewer dashboard. A verdict can pass or fail with a confidence score and notes, and a passing case can be promoted into the golden dataset so the system gets permanently better.

5 Corrections — when users teach the bot

A downvote is not the only signal. When someone corrects the bot in the chat ("no, that's not right — …"), the correction is detected by a fast pre-filter and then classified by an LLM, and logged into the same review pipeline. Feedback becomes signal instead of noise.

6 The Quality Dashboard

All of it is visible in the developer dashboard: every AI call logged with its prompt, response, tokens and latency; the judge's scores; the golden-dataset run history; the human-review queue; the user corrections; and the RAG knowledge base the bot draws on.

7 From Evals to Model Refinement (QLoRA)

The eval and human-review loop does more than catch bad responses: it produces a stream of labelled data — the raw material for refining the model itself.

Five sources already feed it: AI logs, user ratings, conversational corrections, human-review verdicts, and the golden dataset. The plan is to turn them into a training set and run a lightweight QLoRA / LoRA fine-tune on a small open model (Qwen2.5-7B) — small enough to run on a single GPU, and cheap enough to retrain repeatedly.

A model only ships if it clears the same evals as the current one — and rolls back automatically if it regresses.

Honest status — not implemented. QLoRA refinement is one of the reasons the chatbot can get better, thanks to all the evals work — but it is pending for future versions. It cannot improve the current World Cup: on day one there is no data to train on, and by the time a tournament produces enough rated interactions, the event is over. The pipeline above is a design, not a running system. The near-term moat is prompt engineering, a growing golden dataset, and the human-review signal.

Why design it now: the data sources and the labelling rules have to be captured correctly from day one, or there is nothing to train on later. The eval gate and rollback are what make an automated retraining loop safe — without them, a fine-tune is a gamble, not an improvement.

Part B · In development

GrandeSuite Next — an ERP where AI touches money

The AI reads supplier invoices, suggests accounts and payment decisions, and flags anomalies. Because it touches money, the bar is accuracy and auditability — not just plausibility.

8 Reading Invoices with AI

A supplier invoice arrives as a PDF, a scan or a photo. The system reads it with a two-tier approach: a fast path pulls the text layer out of native PDFs, and a vision model handles scans and images. If the model is unavailable, a heuristic extractor takes over — the flow never dead-ends.

Extraction returns the vendor, invoice number, dates, totals and every line item with its SKU, and links the lines to the purchase order before the three-way match runs.

Per-field confidence. Every field carries a confidence score so the interface can show exactly which values to check. This score is a heuristic — based on whether a field is present and plausible — not a model probability. An honest limitation, stated as such.

Human review. The correction panel places the scanned document beside the extracted values, with per-field confidence badges and the match result in one view. A person confirms or fixes the data; the corrected values become the record of truth.

Same pattern as the chatbot: AI produces output → an automatic check scores it → the uncertain cases go to a human. It is the same discipline, applied to money instead of banter.

9 Payments & the Human Gate

Matching is deliberately not AI. Checking an invoice against what was ordered and received is rules-based and auditable — financial integrity is never left to a model's guess.

The payment recommendations, duplicate detection and anomaly flags are also rules-based (due dates, a cash threshold, vendor averages) — advisory only. The controller reviews the recommendation and approves a payment run; the AI never moves money.

Guardrails: no chatbot. No autonomous decisions. AI can be switched off per customer. Every output is a suggestion a person can override.

10 Evals in Development

The same discipline applies to building the ERP with AI agents:

  • Readiness checks before code — plan, architecture, and design validated against each other before implementation.
  • Three independent reviews per change — a blind reviewer (bugs and security), an edge-case reviewer (every branch and boundary), and an acceptance reviewer (against the spec).
  • ~790 automated tests — tests are written before implementation, and every acceptance criterion maps to at least one test.

11 Lessons & Gaps

Confidence UX (designed, not shipped): for Agente Mundial, above 90% confidence, no indicator; 70–90%, a subtle badge; below 70%, the bot qualifies its answer ("No estoy seguro, pero…"). The judge scores exist and are visible in the developer dashboard, but the user-facing signal is one of the honest gaps still open.

What I'd do differently: start the quality pipeline on day one. Retrofitting the judge and review queue after the chatbot was already in use cost more than building it in from the start.

Documented gaps, not hidden ones: no on-call rotation, no automated retraining, and no automated rollback yet.
