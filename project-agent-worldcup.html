Agente Mundial — a chatbot with seven faces

A personality-driven chatbot that runs the social layer of a World Cup prediction pool: it talks trash, summarises matches, and answers questions in 14 languages — with a quality gate on every message.

Applied to: Worldcup Pool (Agente Mundial) Status: Live Personalities: 7

LLM + RAG 7 Personalities 14 Languages LLM-as-a-Judge Human-in-the-Loop

Every World Cup, someone in the group becomes the admin: they tally the points by hand and write the daily summary. That person has the least fun job in the group. Agente Mundial takes that job instead — it keeps the standings alive, roasts the players, and talks to the group like a friend would.

It is deliberately not a swarm of agents. It is one stateless chatbot with a small set of supporting services — simpler to debug, cheaper to run, and easier to trust.

1 Why This Agent

My friend group runs a prediction pool every World Cup. The bottleneck was always the same: one person had to update the spreadsheet after every match and write a summary to keep the banter going.

So I asked a different question: could an AI take the social role instead? Keep the standings current, summarise how everyone is doing, and — most importantly — roast each of us the way a friend would. Agente Mundial is the answer: instead of reading a static spreadsheet, the group talks to the agent, and it talks back.

2 How It Works

Every message flows through one pipeline, with a quality gate before anything reaches the user:

User Message → RAG Memory → Web Search → Personality Prompt → Groq LLM → Judge Eval → Transcreation → Response

Around that pipeline, supporting services do the agent-like work:

  • Personality prompt — each character has a structured humour "anchor" (mechanism, tone, energy, catchphrases, forbidden patterns) injected into the system prompt.
  • Judge — a second, smaller model scores every reply for language purity and quality; failures regenerate with the judge's feedback, up to three times.
  • Transcreation — adapts humour and football idioms across 14 languages, including 8 non-Latin scripts.
  • RAG memory + web search + RSS — conversational context and live match news.
  • Correction detection — when a user corrects the bot, it is detected and logged as a reviewable signal.

The honest detail: it is one stateless chatbot plus services — not a multi-agent system. That choice is the point: fewer moving parts, lower cost, easier to reason about. The judge and human-review machinery is covered in detail on the Evals & Human in the Loop page.

3 The Seven Personalities

Each character is defined by a humour anchor, not just a name. The anchor fixes the comedic mechanism, the energy level, the cultural references, a set of catchphrases to rotate, and a list of forbidden topics.

Andrés Montes
Street wisdom · ES · Energy 5/5
Exuberant, streetwise, epic. Catchphrases like "ráfaga", "toma toma toma", "faroliyo", "jugón". Cultural fuel: street basketball, boxing, salsa, the market.
Josep Pedrerol
Dramatic reveal · ES · Energy 4/5
The TV host with an exclusive. "Atentos", "señores", "lo que les voy a contar". Built on the late-night football debate show.
Tomás Roncero
Exaggerated emotion · ES · Energy 5/5
Everything is historic or an insurmountable abyss. "Esto es histórico", "estoy llorando", "Dios mío". Passionate sports journalism.
Darth Vader
Menacing wit · EN · Energy 3/5
Solemn, imperial, deadpan. "Impressive", "I find your lack of faith disturbing", "your destiny". Star Wars lore and villain monologues.
Donald Trump
Superlatives · EN · Energy 5/5
Everything is the best or a total disaster. "Tremendous", "believe me", "HUGE", "nobody knows". Bombastic, self-congratulatory, parody only.
Fabrizio Romano
Breaking news · ES · Energy 3/5
Predictions are transfers. "Here we go", "breaking", "done deal", "verbal agreement". Transfer-market journalism.
Juez Dredd
Authoritarian judgment · ES · Energy 3/5
The ruthless judge. "I am the law", "sentenciado", "caso cerrado", "cadena perpetua". Mega-City One courtroom humour.

Four characters generate natively in Spanish and two in English; the rest of the 14 languages are reached through transcreation. Users pick their agent's personality from their profile — the same group can hear a different voice for every player.

5 Staying in Character & Safe

  • Structured anchors, not vibes. Every personality encodes its comedic mechanism, energy and forbidden topics, so the bot does not drift into generic or offensive territory.
  • Forbidden patterns. Each anchor lists what it must never do (profanity, dark humour, real politics, real religions).
  • A judge on every reply. Language purity and quality are scored 0–10; a contaminated or low-quality response is regenerated with feedback.
  • Never silently shipped. After three failed attempts a response is force-approved and flagged for human review — never dropped without a trace.
  • Corrections become signal. When a user corrects the bot, the correction is detected, classified, and logged into the review pipeline.
