There is no single best model — there is a best model for each job at each price point. I run a portfolio: fast and cheap where volume is high, stronger where judgment matters, and small where the economics demand it.

And I don't just use models — I use them to build. AI agents wrote the ERP's code under specs, tests, and reviews.

1 The Models I Actually Use

The chatbot's voice. Inference speed gives Agente Mundial its real-time feel, with strong quality for structured tasks.

The judge. Scores every chatbot response for language purity and quality — small, fast, and cheap enough to run on everything.

The failover when Groq is unavailable, so the chatbot keeps responding rather than failing hard.

Llama 3.2 11B Vision on HuggingFace OCR

Reads scanned supplier invoices in the ERP, escalating to human review when confidence is low.

2 The Cascading Strategy

Frontier quality for everything is a margin killer. The direction is to cascade traffic by value — but today, everything runs on free tiers, and the cascade is the plan for when it scales:

  • A self-hosted small model would handle ~96% of traffic — pennies per call, no vendor lock-in.
  • A premium async model would handle the ~4% where quality pays: summaries and analysis.

The break-even math: a $4.99 per-group pass against $0.30–0.50 of AI cost per group per tournament. The cascade is what would make 90%+ margins possible at consumer prices — the free tiers are what make the MVP free today.

Honest framing: the 96/4 cascade and the self-hosted model are targets, not today's reality. Today, 100% of chatbot traffic runs on Groq's free tier, with a HuggingFace fallback.

3 Cost Engineering

  • Free tiers as a product decision: the MVP runs inference on free-tier providers — document reading, setup suggestions, payment-batch composition — at $0 infrastructure cost.
  • Unit economics per feature: costs are tracked honestly per feature rather than hand-waved as "cheap."
  • Funded targets vs. MVP reality: full-scale figures are goals for after revenue; the MVP lives inside free-tier limits by design.
  • Provider abstraction: an inference adapter layer means models can be swapped — Groq to HuggingFace to a future self-hosted model — without code changes.

4 Agentic Coding in Practice

The ERP's codebase is implemented by AI agents across the full MVP — under human-owned product decisions.

  • Specs first: every work item starts from a written requirement with acceptance criteria.
  • Test-first: tests define "done" before implementation starts.
  • Three independent reviews per change: blind, edge-case, and acceptance.
  • Humans own: scope, priorities, framing, and every acceptance decision.

5 What I Learned

  • Latency is a product feature. The chatbot's real-time feel comes from inference speed, not raw model size.
  • Some decisions should never be AI. The ERP's invoice matching is deliberately rules-based and auditable — AI extracts and suggests; deterministic logic decides.
  • Free tiers change product economics. Per-invoice AI cost is what makes competing tools expensive; free-tier inference removes it.
  • Models are swappable. Architecture should depend on capabilities and interfaces, not on one provider's name.
